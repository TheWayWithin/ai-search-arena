## The 10-Hour Pipeline Nobody Sees

Running a benchmark cycle isn't clicking a button. Here's what actually happens:

**Phase 1: Enrollment & Planning**
Every active tool gets enrolled into the cycle. 32 tools this round. Each tool maps to one of 7 market segments (Enterprise SEO, Content Marketing, Local SEO, etc.) so the rankings reflect what matters for YOUR use case, not just a generic "best overall."

**Phase 2: Evaluation (The Long Part)**
Each tool gets evaluated across 51 capability dimensions by 6 independent AI models — GPT-5.2, Claude Sonnet 4.6, Gemini 3 Flash, Grok 4.1 Fast, DeepSeek V3.2, and Mistral Large 3.

That's 32 tools x 51 dimensions x 6 models = **9,792 individual API calls**. Over 350,000 tokens. About 10 hours of continuous processing.

Why six models? Same reason academic peer review uses multiple reviewers. No single model's bias can dominate the results. We take the **median** score — you'd need to compromise 4 out of 6 models to game a ranking.

**Phase 3: Synthesis**
Raw scores get synthesised into composite scores. Confidence tags derived from inter-model agreement. Dense ranking applied (tied scores get the same rank — no artificial separation).

**Phase 4: Publication**
Rankings generated. Badges awarded (Best Overall, segment leaders, Most Improved). The entire evaluation dataset gets sealed with a SHA-256 hash for auditability. Anyone can verify we didn't change the numbers after the fact.

## What Breaks When You Actually Do This

Theory is clean. Production is messy.

During this cycle, our synthesis stage hit a connection pool exhaustion issue. After 9,792 evaluations completed successfully over 10 hours, the process hung during the synthesis-to-composites transition. Zero CPU. No error messages. Just... silence.

Root cause: when resuming a partially-completed cycle, the pipeline was re-upserting 1,632 synthesis records unnecessarily, overwhelming our serverless PostgreSQL connection pool. The fix was making the synthesis stage idempotent — check if records already exist before re-processing. Resume time went from "hang indefinitely" to 44.7 seconds.

This is the kind of operational detail that matters if you're building production data pipelines and why I'm sharing it. Building in public means showing the ugly parts too.

## What Multi-Cycle Data Unlocks

Here's what gets interesting now that we have two cycles of data:

### Trend Tracking
Is a tool improving or declining? With a single cycle, you can't know. With two, you can start to see. With a weekly cadence, you'll have statistically significant trend lines within a month.

### Movement Detection
Which tools moved up or down in the rankings between cycles? Did BrightEdge maintain its #1 position? Did any newcomer break into the top 5? These are questions you can only answer with multiple data points.

### Confidence Building
Each additional cycle strengthens the statistical confidence of our scores. A tool that ranks #3 once might be noise. A tool that ranks #3 across four consecutive cycles is a pattern.

### Segment Stability
Do the segment leaders (Best for Enterprise, Best for Content Marketing) stay consistent? Or does the landscape shift? Multi-cycle data reveals whether recommendations are stable enough to act on.
